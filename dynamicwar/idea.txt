DYNAMIC WAR — IDEAS

Core concept
- A strategy game inspired by Hearts of Iron, but without fixed provinces, hexes, or capture zones.
- Armies project control through their vision and presence. Territory follows the military front dynamically.
- Borders should look soft and irregular rather than revealing the underlying influence grid.
- Use offline OpenStreetMap-derived geography as the base map.

Current bots-first demo
- Three autonomous factions contest a compact Benelux map.
- Faction relations start at hold, warm to tense probes, then war. Combat only fires after war.
- Units space out, hold standoff ranges, and form visible fronts instead of instantly charging.
- A low-resolution influence field turns unit vision into colored territorial control.
- Captures use delay and hysteresis so borders do not flicker.
- Seeded simulations, pause, speed controls, reset, camera follow, and a minimap are available.

Next gameplay improvements
1. Make armies form clearer front lines instead of dense overlapping swarms. (started)
2. Add collision/separation so friendly units spread out and avoid stacking. (started)
3. Replace round influence blobs with terrain-aware propagation and connected front contours.
4. Add movement costs for roads, rivers, cities, coastlines, and difficult terrain.
5. Give bots strategic objectives such as cities, crossings, ports, and encirclement routes.
6. Add reinforcement and supply lines; isolated units should lose strength and control.
7. Distinguish scouts, infantry, armor, artillery, and headquarters by vision and combat role.
8. Add fog of war so factions only act on currently observed enemies and remembered positions.
9. Let the player select a faction, issue move/front-line orders, or remain in spectator mode.
10. Add scenario setup: faction count, unit count, spawn areas, victory share, and map selection.

Border and map improvements
- Smooth the visible control mask with marching squares or signed-distance contours.
- Show a thin contested-front line where opposing influence is nearly equal.
- Keep legal national borders subtle and separate from military control.
- Split larger maps into loadable regional chunks before attempting a global map.
- For a world version, use simplified vector tiles and simulation levels of detail so distant wars run cheaply.

Later systems
- Casualties, manpower, morale, organization, production, diplomacy, alliances, and scripted scenarios.
- Naval transport and amphibious landings so separated landmasses can participate.
- Air reconnaissance and air control as additional influence layers.
- Replay/export support using only the map, seed, scenario, and command history.

Keep the next version focused: improve front shape, unit spacing, strategic objectives, and supply before adding a large economy or global map.
