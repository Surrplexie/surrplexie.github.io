Capsim plan

Playable

Capsim is a sandbox economy. The player is not a trader. The player watches one to one thousand bots compete for net worth, and can change time, money, rates, law, and news. There is no scripted winner.

The game is at /capsim/. This page is the spec. The browser build follows it in simplified form: prices move through the day, the books close once per in-game day, charts keep that daily history, and an optional API only chooses from the same actions the rules can already take.

Titan lock

Ordinary noise does not pull a bot who has already won back to the pack. A fortune becomes real only if the strategy and the circumstances produce it. After that it stays, unless the bot messes up in a major way, falls off over a long stretch, or the player punishes them.

  • $1 million opens a company, margin, and small alliances. A bad year can still erase it.
  • $1 billion opens several companies, lobbying, hostile bids, and the policy of refusing poor counterparties. Rank among the rich sticks unless losses persist.
  • $100 billion is the locked tier. Only a major mess-up, a long fall-off, real exposure to a shock, or the player breaks it.

Day-to-day prices still move, including for titans. What the lock stops is a quiet drift back to the average bot. Chaos difficulty makes a real mistake or a real exposure more likely. It does not add a magnet toward the middle.

Time

At 1×, one real second is two in-game hours. 2×, 3×, and 4× multiply that. Pause, rewind, and skip years are separate. A 365-day year is about 73 minutes at 1× and about 18 minutes at 4×. Skip years resolves whole days, writes the history, and labels the jump. Rewind restores a recent daily snapshot or a god action and cuts that branch’s future.

Bots

Each bot is a capital owner. The public is mood, welfare, and customer headlines, not another crowd of agents. Bots put their own survival and net worth first.

  • Starting wealth is a skewed draw from $1 into the millions, shaped by the scenario. A flat number or a line per bot replaces the roll. Typed wealth can place a titan on day one.
  • Temperaments: ruthless, cautious, innovative, manipulative, opportunistic, cooperative, paranoid. Weights drift after long winning or losing streaks.
  • Risk appetite is conservative, speculative, or extreme leverage.
  • Private goals start as become richest, dominate a sector, bankrupt someone, or just survive. Near ruin, survival takes over.

A decision pass reads survival, news, rivals, firms, the portfolio, then influence. The reason is stored. Rules are the default. Advanced mode is a wider local pass. A local or API model may pick from that same menu, with a daily request cap, a token budget, and a rules fallback. The model does not invent cash or prices.

Markets, firms, and banks

  • Stocks, ETFs, a mutual pool, a broad index, bonds, and CDs. Each has a chart from day one.
  • Companies live in technology, energy, retail, banking, healthcare, manufacturing, or entertainment. Demand, capacity, approval, and competition set sales. A bad launch can be boycotted.
  • Bots can found firms, launch products, take one over when they are large enough, or be blocked by strict antitrust.
  • Loans need a bank with liquidity and enough credibility. Regulation caps leverage. Unpayable interest compounds. Collateral stress forces selling.
  • Shorts profit when a price falls and can be forced shut when it rises. Position size is capped so a one-cent quote cannot create a fantasy share count.
  • Banks take deposits, lend, and can fail. A failure hits that bank’s depositors and the bots tied to its loans.
  • Wealthy bots remember slights, ally, share a tip, plant a rumor, or refuse a bot they think is a bad bet. A refused bot can be let back in after a real improvement.

God tools

  • News scored 0–100, aimed at the economy, an industry, an asset, a company, or one bot, as a short impulse, a fade, or a persistent fact.
  • Create or destroy a dollar amount at the selected bot, or expand and contract the broad money supply.
  • Set the benchmark rate for a number of days. Set regulation from an open market to strict law.
  • Reward or punish the selected bot with cash, debt forgiveness, a fine, seized assets, a reputation hit, or forced bankruptcy.
  • Every manual action is logged with the before and after on mood and the richest fortune. Later archive lines can point back at that action.

Observer mode leaves pause, speed, rewind, and the archive, and turns the levers off. Fog hides private books and secret goals until a scandal or a fine reveals them. Net worth stays visible either way. There is no required victory. Optional stops are a wealth goal or an economic collapse. Saves replay the seed plus the god log so a branch is another intervention on the same start.

What you can read

  • A live board of net worth, cash, debt, assets, and lifetime profit.
  • Charts from day one for net worth, prices, the rate, mood, and the top 1% share, with a log scale.
  • A bot page with temperament, companies, allies, rivals, and the reasons they stored.
  • A news feed, a searchable archive, and lifetime records for the richest fortune, the best day, the worst day, and the largest bankruptcy.
  • Public meters for mood, employment, wages, affordability, availability, approval, and living standard, plus the share held by the richest 1%.
